# Finance and investor relations

## Who they are

A VP Finance or CFO, an FP&A lead, and at a smaller company the founder who does both. At a bootstrapped or seed-stage company the CEO owns investor relations personally. They are measured on forecast accuracy, cash runway, gross margin, net revenue retention, CAC payback and whether the board and investors trust the numbers. They sit next to sales on pricing, discounts and pipeline, next to the CEO on the board pack and the fundraise.

## Responsibilities

**Planning and forecasting**
- Annual plan, quarterly reforecast, monthly budget vs actual
- Revenue forecast from pipeline: coverage by segment, conversion by stage, win rates by segment and source
- Headcount and spend plans for GTM, with the business case for each increment

**Pricing, packaging and deal governance**
- Price list and packaging reviews, usually yearly, with sales and product marketing
- Discount policy and approval matrix; deal desk review of non-standard deals (pricing, terms, margin impact, competitive context)
- Revenue recognition for bundles, one-time fees and multi-year terms

**Board and investor reporting**
- Monthly or quarterly board pack: financials, SaaS metrics, pipeline, GTM efficiency, competitive position, risks
- Investor updates between rounds; the fundraise itself: pitch deck, data room, due diligence questionnaire (50 to 200 questions across market, competition, customers, financials, legal, team)
- Tracking what investors keep asking and which objections resurface

**Market and competitive inputs to the model**
- Market sizing (TAM, SAM, SOM) and the assumptions behind growth
- Competitor benchmarks: pricing, packaging, funding, analyst standing, where deals are won and lost

## Recurring activities

| Cadence | Activity |
|---|---|
| Daily | Deal desk approvals (24-hour SLA is the common target), cash position |
| Weekly | Pipeline review with sales leadership, discount exceptions |
| Monthly | Close, budget vs actual, forecast refresh, investor update |
| Quarterly | Board pack, reforecast, pricing exceptions review, segment win-rate review |
| Yearly | Annual plan, price list and packaging review, GTM budget cases |
| Per round | Pitch deck, data room, due diligence answers, competitor benchmarks |

## Where Calven fits

Half of what finance presents is not financial: why deals are won and lost, whether the pipeline is in profile, what buyers said about price, how the company is positioned against the competitors investors will ask about. That half lives in Calven, approved by the people who own it, with sample sizes attached. The financial half (ARR, cash, margin, CAC) stays in the finance stack, and every page here says so where a step needs it.
