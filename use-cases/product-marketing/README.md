# Product marketing

## Who they are

One to five people: a Director or VP of Product Marketing, one or two PMMs, sometimes a competitive intelligence or customer marketing specialist. They report to the CMO, occasionally to product. They are measured on win rate, pipeline influenced by launches, sales adoption of the story, content use by the field, and whether the company tells one consistent story. In 44% of companies the team is one or two people, and the rest of the company treats them as the people who know the market, the buyer and the product.

## Responsibilities

**Market and customer intelligence**
- Build and maintain the ideal customer profile and the buyer, stakeholder and user personas
- Run or read win/loss, customer interviews, call recordings and surveys
- Track competitors: pricing, launches, messaging, where deals are won and lost against each
- Watch market trends and analyst coverage, brief leadership on what they mean

**Positioning and messaging**
- Own the positioning framework: category, alternatives, differentiation, proof
- Own the messaging framework: narrative, pillars, value props by persona, the persona × stage matrix, objection handling
- Keep the product brief current: capabilities, integrations, pricing and packaging
- Audit website, decks and campaigns for drift from the approved story

**Launches**
- Set the launch tier, write the launch messaging, build the launch kit
- Brief marketing, sales, success and support before the launch
- Measure adoption and pipeline after it

**Sales enablement content**
- Battlecards, one-pagers, solution briefs, pitch decks, demo storylines, sales FAQs
- Customer evidence: quotes, proof points, case studies, reference stories
- Answer the field's questions on competitors, pricing and product

**Pricing and packaging input**
- Bring buyer price feedback, competitor pricing and win/loss commercial drivers into packaging decisions

## Recurring activities

| Cadence | Activity |
|---|---|
| Daily | Answer questions from sales and marketing; review copy for accuracy; read new calls and signals |
| Weekly | Competitive update to the field; messaging review of campaigns in flight; sync with product on what ships |
| Monthly | Win/loss readout; battlecard refresh; content and claims audit of key pages |
| Quarterly | Persona and ICP refresh; positioning review; competitive teardown; analyst briefings; launch planning for next quarter |
| Per launch | Messaging, launch kit, enablement session, post-launch readout |

## Where Calven fits

Every PMM deliverable is a synthesis of the company's market, buyer, product and deal knowledge. Calven's agents keep that knowledge current and the PMM approves it. From the AI tool, MCP turns the approved Universe into the deliverable: a battlecard distributed as a one-pager, a persona brief for a new campaign, a win/loss readout for leadership, a fact-check of a page before it ships. What stays with the PMM is judgment: which story to tell, what to approve, what to say no to. Creative production, design and publishing stay in the tools the team already uses.
