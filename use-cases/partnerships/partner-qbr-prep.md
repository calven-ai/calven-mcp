# Partner QBR prep

**Team:** Partnerships · also revenue operations, sales leadership
**Impact:** Medium. A quarterly business review that brings the partner the market read, the competitive moves and the win/loss evidence in their segment is worth attending. One that only shows pipeline numbers is a status call.
**Prerequisites:** CRM connected (deals by lead source, segment and competitor), win/loss surveys running, competitors tracked. Better with market research run (trends in the partner's segment) and call transcripts ingested.

## What the team is trying to do

Prepare the QBR with a partner: what happened last quarter in their deals, why deals were won and lost, what competitors did, what the market is doing in their segment, where enablement is thin, and the plan for next quarter. Done means a deck or brief the partner learns from and a joint plan with named accounts. The pipeline numbers come from the PRM or CRM; the "why" and the market context come from Calven.

## The work, end to end

| # | Step | What the team does | Where Calven helps | Pulls from |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Pull the numbers | Partner-sourced and influenced pipeline, closed deals | Deals with the partner as lead source, if the CRM records it | CRM deals |
| 2 | Explain the outcomes | Why their deals were won or lost | Deal drivers and loss reasons on those deals, with the buyer's words | Deal drivers, survey responses, CRM deals |
| 3 | Competitive read | Which competitors appeared and what they did | Win rate per competitor in the partner's segment; competitive signals this quarter | Competitive intelligence dashboard, competitive signals |
| 4 | Market read | What changed in the partner's segment | Trends and opportunities in the segment, analyst findings | Trends, market opportunities, analyst findings |
| 5 | Enablement gaps | Where the partner's reps struggled | Objections raised in their deals that the talk track does not cover; messaging pull-through if their calls are ingested | Deal drivers, messaging dashboard |
| 6 | Target accounts | Who to go after together next quarter | Accounts in the partner's segment or region by ICP tier, not yet worked or lost long ago | CRM accounts, deals |
| 7 | Build the brief or deck | Assemble | A draft in QBR order | All of the above |
| 8 | Run the QBR, agree the plan | Meeting, actions | Calven does not help here | |

## Recommended prompts

### Step 2 to 4: the "why" and the context

```
Using Calven MCP, prepare the market and win/loss section of the QBR with [partner] for [quarter].

CONTEXT
The partner sells into [segment] in [region]. I have their pipeline numbers. I need why deals were won and lost, what competitors did, and what the market did, for their segment.

PULL FROM THE UNIVERSE
- Deals in [segment] in [quarter] with lead source [partner] if recorded, otherwise all deals in [segment]: outcomes, loss reasons, competitors.
- Deal drivers on those deals: top win and loss reasons with evidence quotes.
- The competitive performance read for [quarter]: win rate per competitor with n; competitive signals in the quarter.
- Trends and opportunities touching [segment].

BUILD
- Why we won and why we lost in this segment, with quotes.
- The competitors that mattered and what they did.
- The two market shifts the partner should know.
- The one enablement gap the loss reasons point at.

OUTPUT
A QBR section in that order, with sources and n.

GROUNDING
Numbers from the dashboards only, with n and window. If partner-sourced deals are not tagged in the CRM, say so and use the segment view, labelled.

[name the partner, segment, region and quarter]
```

### Step 6: joint target accounts

```
Using Calven MCP, list the accounts [partner] and we should work together next quarter.

CONTEXT
The partner covers [segment] in [region]. I want accounts in profile that nobody is working, or that we lost long enough ago to retry.

PULL FROM THE UNIVERSE
- CRM accounts in [segment] and [region] by ICP tier.
- Deals on those accounts: none, open, or closed-lost more than 6 months ago with the loss reason.
- Contacts known at each.

BUILD
- A table: account, tier, status (never worked / lost on [reason] on [date]), known contact, suggested angle.

OUTPUT
The ranked table with sources.

GROUNDING
Use only CRM data in the Universe. Exclude accounts with an open deal. If contact names are withheld, give the role.

[name the partner, segment and region]
```

### Step 5: enablement gap from their deals

```
Using Calven MCP, which objections came up in [partner]'s deals that our partner talk track does not cover?

CONTEXT
I want to fix the enablement kit before next quarter.

PULL FROM THE UNIVERSE
- Deal drivers that hurt us on deals with lead source [partner] (or in [segment]), grouped.
- The objection handling section of our messaging.

BUILD
- A list: objection, how often, whether the messaging covers it, suggested fix.

OUTPUT
The list with sources.

GROUNDING
Count only recorded drivers. Flag uncovered objections for the PMM rather than writing responses.

[name the partner or segment]
```

## Ad hoc questions

- What were the top loss reasons in [segment] last quarter?
- What is our win rate against [competitor] in [segment], with n?
- What did [competitor] change last quarter?
- Which trends touch [segment] right now?
- Which Tier 1 accounts in [region] has nobody worked?
- Which deals in [segment] did we lose on price, and what did buyers say?
- Are partner-sourced deals tagged by lead source in our CRM?
- Which objections hurt us most in [segment] deals?
- Which accounts lost more than six months ago on [reason] are worth a retry?
- What proof point from [segment] can I show the partner?

## Good practice

- Bring the "why" and the market, not only the numbers. That is what the partner cannot get elsewhere.
- Label the view. If the CRM does not tag partner-sourced deals, show the segment view and say so.
- Turn one loss reason into one enablement fix per QBR. More than that does not get done.
- Leave with named accounts and a known contact for each.

## Not covered today

- Partner pipeline from the PRM, commissions, program metrics.
- Partner-sourced deals absent from the CRM mirror.
- The meeting, the actions, the follow-up.
- Partner satisfaction and NPS.
