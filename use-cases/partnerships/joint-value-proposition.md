# Joint value proposition

**Team:** Partnerships (alliances) · also product marketing, partner marketing, product management
**Impact:** High. A technology or integration partnership only sells if both sides can say what the two products do together for a shared buyer. The joint value proposition is the document every co-marketing asset, marketplace listing and co-sell pitch is written from.
**Prerequisites:** strategy documents approved (positioning, messaging, product brief with integrations), personas approved. Better with market research run (the trend behind the partnership) and call transcripts ingested (customers who use both products).

## What the team is trying to do

Agree with a partner a joint value proposition: the shared buyer, the problem the two products solve together that neither solves alone, the combined workflow, the proof, and the messaging each side will use. Done means one page both companies sign off on. The alliance manager typically drafts the company's half from positioning and then negotiates; the draft needs to be on-positioning and true to the integration as the product brief describes it.

## The work, end to end

| # | Step | What the team does | Where Calven helps | Pulls from |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Define the shared buyer | Which persona and segment both companies sell to | The ICP and personas; which persona's canvas mentions the partner's category among jobs or tools | ICP, personas |
| 2 | Name the problem | The job the two products do together | Persona pains and jobs; customer quotes about the workflow or the partner's product | Personas, quotes, themes |
| 3 | Describe the integration | What actually connects and how | The product brief integrations and technical architecture sections | Product brief |
| 4 | Frame the why now | The market shift behind the partnership | Trends and opportunities that touch the partner's category | Trends, market opportunities, analyst findings |
| 5 | Write our half | Positioning-consistent statement and messages | Positioning value themes and messaging pillars applied to the joint story | Positioning, messaging |
| 6 | Add proof | Customers using both, outcomes | Quotes and conversations mentioning the partner or the workflow | Quotes, customer conversations |
| 7 | Negotiate and agree | Merge with the partner's half | Calven does not help here | |
| 8 | Check for conflict | Make sure the joint story does not contradict our positioning or a competitor stance | Positioning drift check; whether the partner also partners with a tracked competitor | Positioning, competitors, competitive signals |
| 9 | Cascade | Marketplace listing, co-marketing, enablement | See co-marketing-briefs.md and partner-enablement-kit.md | |

## Recommended prompts

### Step 1 to 5: the draft joint value proposition

```
Using Calven MCP, draft our half of a joint value proposition with [partner], whose product does [what it does].

CONTEXT
We are a [technology / integration] partner. The shared buyer is probably [persona] in [segment]. I need a draft on our positioning that is true to the integration as our product brief describes it.

PULL FROM THE UNIVERSE
- The ICP and the [persona] canvas: jobs, pains, objections.
- The product brief: the integration with [partner's product] if listed, the technical architecture, the use cases it serves.
- Our positioning: value themes, unique attributes; our messaging pillars.
- Trends or opportunities that touch [partner's category].
- Customer quotes that mention [partner], the workflow, or the combined job.

BUILD
- The shared buyer and the problem, in the buyer's words.
- What the two products do together that neither does alone, grounded in the integration the brief describes.
- Why now, from a tracked trend.
- Three joint messages, each tied to one of our pillars.
- Proof from customers, if any.

OUTPUT
A one-page draft with sources, plus a list of what I need from the partner's side.

GROUNDING
Use only the Universe for our side. If the brief does not list this integration, say so and describe only what the architecture section supports. Do not invent what the partner's product does; mark it for the partner to supply.

[name the partner and what their product does]
```

### Step 3: what the integration actually does

```
Using Calven MCP, what does our product brief say about the integration with [partner's product]?

CONTEXT
I am about to claim things in a joint document and need the exact scope.

PULL FROM THE UNIVERSE
- The product brief integrations and technical architecture sections.
- Product changes in the last 180 days touching this integration.

ANSWER
- What the integration does, what it does not, any limits, and whether it changed recently.

OUTPUT
A short factual note with the brief section cited.

GROUNDING
Only the brief. If it does not mention the integration, say "not in the brief".

[name the partner's product]
```

### Step 6: customers who use both

```
Using Calven MCP, which customers mention [partner's product] or [the combined workflow] on calls?

CONTEXT
I want proof for the joint story and candidates for a joint case study.

PULL FROM THE UNIVERSE
- Quotes mentioning [partner's product] or [workflow], with account, sentiment and date.
- Conversations where the topic came up.

BUILD
- A table: account, what they said, sentiment, whether it is a candidate for a case study.

OUTPUT
The table with sources, or "nothing recorded".

GROUNDING
Quote verbatim. Do not infer that an account uses the partner's product unless they said so.

[name the partner's product or the workflow]
```

### Step 8: conflict check

```
Using Calven MCP, check this joint value proposition against our positioning and our competitive stance.

CONTEXT
Below is the agreed draft with [partner]. Before legal and marketing sign off I want to know where it drifts from our positioning or creates a competitive conflict.

PULL FROM THE UNIVERSE
- Our positioning and messaging.
- The competitors we track and any signal that [partner] partners with one of them.

CHECK
- Lines that adopt a category frame or claim we do not use.
- Claims about our product the brief does not support.
- Any competitive conflict the signals reveal.

OUTPUT
The draft annotated, then the on-positioning version.

GROUNDING
Judge only against the Universe. If the draft is clean, say so.

[paste the draft]
```

## Ad hoc questions

- Which of our personas has [partner's category] in their jobs or tools?
- What does the product brief say about our integration with [partner's product]?
- Which trend explains why a [partner's category] partnership matters now?
- Do any customers mention [partner's product] on calls?
- Which of our pillars does a joint story with [partner] support best?
- Is [partner] mentioned in any competitive signal?
- What is the approved way to describe our category next to a [partner's category] product?
- Which segment do we and [partner] both sell to, per our ICP?
- What proof points can appear in a joint one-pager?
- Did the integration with [partner's product] change recently?

## Good practice

- Start from the persona, not the integration. A joint value proposition about an API is not one.
- State only what the brief says the integration does. Overclaiming on a joint document damages two brands.
- Tie each joint message to one of your pillars. If it does not fit a pillar, it is the partner's message, not a joint one.
- Run the conflict check after the partner's edits, not before.

## Not covered today

- The partner's product, roadmap and positioning. They supply their half.
- Marketplace listing submission, co-sell tooling, legal review.
- Usage data on the integration.
- Changing our positioning to fit a partner. That is a PMM decision made in Calven.
