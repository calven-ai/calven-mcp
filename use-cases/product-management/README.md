# Product management


## Who they are

Product managers, group PMs and the head of product. They own the outcome of a product or area: what gets built, why, in which order, and whether it moved the number. Measured on adoption, retention and revenue of their area, on-time delivery of what they committed to, and the quality of the decisions behind the roadmap. They sit between engineering and design on one side and sales, customer success and marketing on the other. In a company with no PMM, the PM also owns launch messaging and sales enablement for their area.

## Responsibilities

**Strategy and roadmap**
- Translate company strategy into product objectives and an outcome-driven roadmap, refreshed monthly, with a 3-month detailed and 6-month directional view.
- Prioritise with a framework (RICE, weighted scoring, cost of delay) and defend the trade-offs to leadership, sales and customers.
- Own pricing and packaging input for their area: which tier a capability lands in, what moves to a higher plan, what the market pays.

**Discovery**
- Talk to customers every week (the product trio of PM, designer and engineer), map opportunities to the outcome, test assumptions before building.
- Synthesise feedback from calls, win/loss, support and sales into opportunities, not feature lists.
- Know the competitors' products in detail: where they are ahead, where they are not, and whether buyers care.

**Definition and delivery**
- Write the problem statement, PRD or opportunity brief with customer evidence, success metrics and scope.
- Run backlog grooming, sprint planning and release management with engineering.
- Name features, set beta criteria, recruit beta customers, decide when something is ready.

**Launch and lifecycle**
- Lead launch readiness across product, marketing, sales, support and legal: scope, messaging, enablement, documentation, release notes.
- Keep the field informed: sales FAQs, what the product does and does not do, what changed.
- Manage deprecations and migrations with a message map for customers and internal teams.

**Stakeholders**
- Prepare and run customer advisory board sessions for strategic input, not feature voting.
- Report to leadership on outcomes, risks and what customers are saying.

## Recurring activities

| Cadence | Activity |
|---|---|
| Daily | Stand-up, backlog questions from engineering, sales and CS questions about what the product does |
| Weekly | Customer interview, product trio discovery session, roadmap and OKR check, triage of incoming feedback |
| Monthly | Roadmap refresh and share-out, product review with leadership, release notes for what shipped |
| Quarterly | Roadmap retrospective (planned vs shipped), OKR planning, pricing and packaging review, customer advisory board, competitive review |
| Per launch | Opportunity brief, PRD, beta program, naming, launch readiness, sales FAQ, release notes, post-launch review |
| Per deprecation | Impact assessment, customer and internal message map, migration plan |

## Where Calven fits

The PM's hardest arguments are about evidence: which gap is costing deals, whether buyers care about a competitor feature, what customers actually say about a problem, and whether a launch claim is true. Calven holds that evidence as the company approved it: product gaps ranked by deals at stake, deal drivers with the buyer's words, customer themes and quotes, competitor dossiers with feature comparisons, pricing verdicts from win/loss, the product brief, and the claims and drift findings that show where published documents went stale. Engineering estimates, usage analytics, support tickets and the roadmap tool stay where they are; Calven supplies the market and customer side of the decision.
