# Legal and procurement

**Use-case pages:** [`use-cases/legal-and-procurement/`](../use-cases/legal-and-procurement/)

## Who they are

General counsel or commercial counsel, a compliance or privacy lead, and whoever runs procurement (often finance or operations in a company under 200 people). They report to the CEO or CFO. They are measured on turnaround time for contracts and reviews, on nothing shipping that creates exposure, and on what the company pays for the tools it buys. In a small B2B software company the same two people review a press release in the morning, a security questionnaire at noon and a vendor order form after lunch.

## Responsibilities

**Marketing and advertising compliance**
- Review external claims before they ship: performance, superlatives ("#1", "fastest"), comparative claims naming a competitor, testimonials and endorsements, pricing statements.
- Keep substantiation on file for every claim at the moment it is published. A claim without proof is not made.
- Review comparative content ("vs" pages, teardowns, competitive ads) against the standard that every statement about a rival is objective, verifiable and current.
- Keep privacy statements, cookie and consent language and data-use claims in marketing consistent with what the company actually does.

**Commercial contracts**
- Own the templates the business sends out: master agreement, order form, DPA, NDA, partner and reseller terms.
- Review and negotiate inbound redlines, and check every order form and proposal for what it promises: plan inclusions, limits, pricing, discounts, integrations, support terms.
- Keep the fallback positions and the approved exceptions in one place so sales does not improvise.

**Buyer due diligence**
- Answer, or coordinate the answer to, security questionnaires, vendor risk assessments and procurement questionnaires from prospects.
- Keep a standard answer bank current and consistent with the product, the policies and the certifications actually held.
- Review what sales and marketing say about security, compliance and data handling so no answer contradicts the collateral.

**Intellectual property**
- Clear product, feature and campaign names before anyone gets attached to them. Ask marketing for three candidates, not one.
- Watch for confusion with competitor names and categories, and for claims that borrow a rival's trademark.

**Procurement of the company's own tools**
- Gather requirements, evaluate vendors, run the security and privacy review, negotiate the contract, track renewals and usage.
- Keep a vendor register with owners, terms, data processed and review dates.

## Recurring activities

- **Daily:** contract redlines, questions from sales ("can we say this", "can we agree to that"), claim checks on copy about to ship.
- **Weekly:** questionnaire responses in flight, vendor contract queue, review of new web pages and campaign assets.
- **Monthly:** vendor renewals, standard-answer bank updates, audit of live claims on the site.
- **Per launch:** release and press copy review, pricing and packaging changes, new names cleared, partner and analyst statements checked.
- **Per enterprise deal:** order form, DPA, security questionnaire, exceptions log.
- **Quarterly:** review of comparative content still live, vendor register review, policy and template refresh.

## Where Calven fits

Legal's recurring question is "can we prove this, and is it still true". Calven holds the company's approved product brief, the register of every claim its documents and site make with proof status, the record of what changed in the product and which documents went stale, and the evidence behind every competitor statement (battlecards, dossiers, signals, win/loss). Reading those from the AI tool turns a claim review from an email chain into a check against the source. Calven does not hold contracts, policies, trademark registries, certifications or legal precedent, so contract drafting, trademark searches and regulatory judgment stay where they are. Nothing here is legal advice; it is a fact check against the company's own approved documents.
