# Sales leadership


## Who they are

The VP of Sales (or CRO) and the front-line sales managers. They own the number: quota attainment, pipeline coverage, forecast accuracy, win rate, deal size and cycle length. They report to the CEO or CRO and present to the board. A manager runs four to ten reps; the VP runs the managers, the operating cadence, hiring, territories and the relationship with marketing, product and finance. Their week is pipeline reviews, forecast calls, deal reviews, one-on-ones and the questions from the CEO that arrive at 4pm.

## Responsibilities

**Running the number**
- Weekly pipeline review: coverage, progression, stalled deals, next steps
- Monthly forecast call: commit and best case per rep, the number that goes to finance and the board
- Deal reviews on the large or at-risk opportunities: who is on it, what could sink it, what to do this week
- Quarterly business reviews and the board update: what happened against plan, win rate and where it moved, pipeline entering the quarter, committed actions

**Running the team**
- One-on-ones split between deals and skills; coaching from real calls, not opinion
- Hiring: scorecards, interview questions, role-plays, territory exercises
- Onboarding and ramp with enablement; sales kickoff content
- Performance management and comp input

**Shaping the plan**
- Territory and quota design with RevOps: segments, capacity, fit
- Pricing and discount feedback to finance and product; deal desk decisions
- Competitive and product gap escalation from the field to product and PMM
- Win/loss readouts: what the buyers said, and what changes because of it

## Recurring activities

| Cadence | Activity |
|---|---|
| Daily | Deal questions from reps; the CEO's question; approvals at the deal desk |
| Weekly | Pipeline review per team; manager one-on-ones; team stand-up; at-risk deal reviews |
| Monthly | Forecast call and sign-off; win/loss readout; hiring pipeline review |
| Quarterly | QBR per rep and per team; board update; territory and quota adjustments; sales kickoff or mid-year refresh; pricing feedback to finance |
| Per hire | Scorecard, interview loop, role-play, offer |
| Per big deal | Deal review, exec sponsor assignment, competitive strategy |

## Where Calven fits

Sales leaders decide on numbers that already exist in the CRM and on reasons that exist only in buyers' heads. Calven MCP puts both behind the leader's own AI tool: win rate by segment, competitor and persona with sample sizes, pipeline in and out of profile, what buyers said decided each deal, how reps actually pitch on calls, and the competitor moves of the month. The forecast call gets a risk read grounded in loss patterns, the coaching conversation gets the rep's own words, the board slide gets numbers with sources. Calven does not hold quota, comp, activity data or the forecast itself; those stay in the CRM and the forecasting tool.
