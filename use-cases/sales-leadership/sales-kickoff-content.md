# Sales kickoff content


You're planning the content for the kickoff or mid-year refresh: the state of the market, the year in win/loss, the competitors, the story we tell, who we sell to. You get an agenda with a brief per session, so enablement and PMM build from the same facts. Calven grounds each session in the current documents and last year's evidence, so the kickoff doesn't recycle last year's deck with new dates.

## Prompts

### Write the kickoff session briefs

```
Using Calven MCP, write the content briefs for our sales kickoff.

CONTEXT
Five content sessions, forty minutes each: state of the market, the year in win/loss, the competitors, our story, who we sell to. Each brief goes to the speaker and to enablement.

PULL FROM THE UNIVERSE
- Approved trends and opportunities with their so-what, and analyst findings.
- The win/loss scoreboard for last year, the top drivers each way, competitor results, product gaps, and five verbatims.
- The battlecards for the Tier 1 competitors and their signals of the year.
- Our positioning statement, messaging pillars and one-liner.
- The ICP tiers and the two or three buyer personas with pains and objections.

BUILD
- Per session: the three points to land, the evidence for each with its source, the one slide that carries it, and the question to leave reps with.

OUTPUT
Five one-page briefs.

GROUNDING
Use only the Universe, cited with n where a rate appears. Verbatim quotes. Do not add market claims the trends do not hold.
```

### Write the kickoff certification quiz

```
Using Calven MCP, write a twenty-question certification quiz for the kickoff.

FILL IN
- Competitors: [the two competitors to cover]

PULL FROM THE UNIVERSE
- Positioning, messaging (pillars, objection handling), ICP (tiers, disqualifiers), product brief (packaging).
- The battlecards for the competitors.

BUILD
- Twenty questions with answers and the document section each comes from: five on the story, five on the ICP and personas, five on competitors, five on the product and packaging.

OUTPUT
The quiz and the answer key.

GROUNDING
Every answer must be in the Universe, cited.
```

### Build the kickoff competitor session

```
Using Calven MCP, build the competitor session for the kickoff.

PULL FROM THE UNIVERSE
- Every Tier 1 competitor: battlecard summary (how we win, where we lose, landmines), win rate against them last year, their top signals of the year.

OUTPUT
One slide per competitor plus a watch list of three moves to expect.

GROUNDING
Use only battlecards, dashboards and signals in the Universe, cited with dates and samples.
```

## Advanced prompts

### Run a competitive war-game tournament

```
Design a war-game tournament for the kickoff: teams of reps play us and the competitor in rounds of move and counter-move, judged against what actually wins. Use Calven MCP for the competitors' playbooks and what decided our real deals.

FILL IN
- Competitors: [two or three competitors]
- Teams: [number of teams and minutes per round]

CONTEXT
Competitor sessions get a slide and a nod. A tournament makes reps argue the competitor's side, which is where they learn the counter.

FROM CALVEN
- Each competitor's battlecard: how they win, where they lose, landmines, objection handling.
- Their signals from the last two quarters.
- Deal drivers from deals against each one, with evidence quotes.
- Our win rate against each, with n.

WAR-GAME
- Build one scenario per competitor from a real, anonymised deal profile: segment, buying group, the stage where it turned.
- Rounds: one team plays us, one plays the competitor. Three rounds (opening pitch, objection, final proposal), two minutes a move.
- Write the judge's sheet: what earns points (a move that matches what decided real deals) and what loses them (a claim the battlecard says backfires).
- Add a twist card per scenario, based on a real signal: the competitor cuts price, launches a feature, hires a known name.

OUTPUT
The scenario packs, the round rules, the judge's sheet with points, the twist cards, and the facilitator's debrief listing the moves that won real deals.

GROUNDING
Scenarios use real deal profiles, anonymised. Twist cards trace to recorded signals. Scoring cites drivers; don't reward a move no deal supports.
```

### Build a branching deal simulation

```
Build a choose-your-path deal simulation for the kickoff from a real deal we lost, where reps make a decision at each stage and see where it leads. Use Calven MCP for the deal and what decides deals like it.

FILL IN
- Deal: [a lost deal to base it on]
- Format: [interactive HTML page, slide deck or printed cards]

CONTEXT
Reps learn more from a deal they can replay than from a talk about it. A branching simulation lets them make the call the real rep made, and then a better one.

FROM CALVEN
- The deal: segment, stages reached, contacts by role, competitor, loss reason, feedback.
- Its deal drivers and evidence quotes.
- Drivers from won deals in the same segment: what the winners did differently.
- The persona canvases for the buying group.

BUILD
- Five decision points, one per stage. Each has three choices: what the real rep did, a common mistake, and what won deals did.
- Each choice leads to the next stage with a consequence drawn from the drivers: the buyer's reaction, a new contact, the competitor's move.
- Score each path. The best path ends in a win; the real path ends in the real loss.
- If you can build an interactive HTML page, do; otherwise write it as numbered cards.

OUTPUT
The simulation file or card set, the scoring, and the debrief: the real path, the winning path and the one decision that separated them.

GROUNDING
Consequences trace to drivers, quotes or canvases, cited. Anonymise the account and the people. Mark any filler as invented for the exercise.
```

## Ad hoc questions

- What are the three approved trends with the biggest so-what for us?
- What were last year's top three win drivers and loss drivers?
- Which competitor moved most last year, and what did they do?
- What is our one-liner and the three pillars?
- Which persona should every rep know cold, and what are their top pains?
- Which product gap cost the most deals last year?
- What is new in the product brief since last kickoff?
- What did buyers say about our sales process last year?
- Which segment should reps prioritise this year according to the ICP?
- Which objection should every rep be able to answer, and the approved line?
- Which won deal last year makes the best kickoff story, with the buyer's quote?
- Which persona's objections changed most since last year?
- Which pillar do reps pull through least, according to the messaging dashboard?
- What did the competitor we lose to most change in the last six months?
- Which claims should reps stop making, according to the claims list?
- Which buying trigger showed up most on last year's won deals?
