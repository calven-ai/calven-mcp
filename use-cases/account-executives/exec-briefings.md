# Exec briefings

**Team:** Account executives · also sales leadership, solutions engineering
**Impact:** Medium. The economic buyer gives the deal one meeting, and the rep usually has never met them.
**Prerequisites:** positioning approved, personas approved (the executive persona), win/loss surveys running. Better with competitors tracked and call transcripts ingested.

## What the team is trying to do

Prepare the one meeting with the executive sponsor or economic buyer: what they care about, the business case in their terms, the competitive read, the proof a peer would believe, and the one ask. Done means a one-page briefing the champion can also use to sell internally. Without the company's own evidence, the exec meeting is the demo again, one level up.

## The work, end to end

| # | Step | What the team does | Where Calven helps | Pulls from |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Read the executive | Their goals, KPIs, what they distrust | The executive persona canvas: goals and KPIs, pains, objections, hooks | Persona canvas |
| 2 | Frame the business case | The problem in company terms, the metric, the cost of inaction | The positioning narrative and value themes; the champion's stated metric from the rep's notes | Positioning document |
| 3 | Position against the alternatives | Why us, why now, the honest trade-off | Positioning: competitive alternatives, why now; the battlecard | Positioning, battlecard |
| 4 | Bring peer proof | What an executive at a similar company said | Quotes from executive-level speakers with quantified outcomes | Quotes (role, highlight) |
| 5 | Anticipate the objection | What this persona asks that kills deals | Objections on the canvas; deciding drivers where the economic buyer was the blocker | Persona canvas, deal drivers |
| 6 | Set the ask | The decision or sponsorship needed | Calven does not help here | |
| 7 | Rehearse | Practise against the persona | Role-play as the executive | Persona canvas |

## Recommended prompts

### Step 1 to 5: the briefing

```
Using Calven MCP, build my briefing for a meeting with [contact], [title] at [account].

CONTEXT
[Contact] is the economic buyer on [deal]. The champion, a [persona], told me the problem is [problem] and the metric is [metric]. [Competitor] is the alternative. I get 25 minutes.

PULL FROM THE UNIVERSE
- The [executive persona] canvas: goals and KPIs, pains, objections, messaging hooks.
- Our positioning: the narrative, value themes, why now, competitive alternatives.
- The [competitor] battlecard: how we win and where we lose at the executive level.
- Two quotes from executives at similar accounts, verbatim, with outcomes.
- The objections from economic buyers that decided lost deals, with n.

BUILD
- What this executive cares about, in three lines.
- The business case in their terms: problem, metric, cost of waiting.
- Why us against [competitor], with the trade-off stated.
- The peer proof.
- The objection they will raise and the answer.
- The one ask.

OUTPUT
A one-page briefing with sources, plus a version the champion can forward.

GROUNDING
Use only the Universe, cited. Do not invent outcomes, numbers or executive reactions. Where the economic-buyer evidence is thin, say so.

[name the contact, title, deal, champion persona, problem, metric and competitor]
```

### Step 7: rehearse the exec meeting

```
Using Calven MCP, role-play [executive persona] in the meeting for [deal].

CONTEXT
Play the economic buyer. They have 25 minutes, were not on the calls, and have seen [competitor]'s proposal.

PULL FROM THE UNIVERSE
- The [executive persona] canvas.
- The [competitor] battlecard.
- The objections economic buyers raised on lost deals.

ROLE-PLAY
- Open as the executive. Interrupt when I go into features. Raise the objections this persona actually has.
- After "debrief": where I lost you, the one thing that would have made you sponsor it.

OUTPUT
An exchange, then a short debrief.

GROUNDING
Stay true to the canvas and the record. Do not make the executive easier than the evidence says.

[name the persona, deal and competitor]
```

### Review mode: check my briefing

```
Using Calven MCP, read my exec briefing as [executive persona].

CONTEXT
Below is the briefing. Tell me what they would skip and what they would doubt.

PULL FROM THE UNIVERSE
- The [persona] canvas, and run the persona review on the text.
- The product brief for any claim.

CHECK
- Lines that are features, not outcomes.
- Claims the brief does not support.
- The question they would still have.

OUTPUT
The briefing annotated with severity, then the three edits.

GROUNDING
Only from the Universe. Say so if it is sound.

[paste the briefing and name the persona]
```

## Ad hoc questions

- What does a [executive persona] care about and distrust?
- What is our positioning narrative in two sentences?
- Why now, according to our positioning?
- Which executive at a similar company said something quotable about the outcome?
- What objections do economic buyers raise that kill our deals?
- How do we win against [competitor] at the executive level?
- What is the cost-of-inaction argument in our messaging?
- What is the hook for a [executive persona]?
- Which value theme matters most to a [executive persona]?
- Where does our win rate drop when the economic buyer is not engaged?

## Good practice

- Paste the champion's problem and metric. The business case is theirs; Calven supplies the framing and the proof.
- Ask for outcomes, not features. The persona review will flag the feature lines.
- Use executive-level quotes. A practitioner's quote proves usability; an executive's proves return.
- Rehearse once. The debrief shows the moment the meeting turns into a demo.

## Not covered today

- The executive's own background, priorities this quarter and public statements. That is the rep's research; paste it in.
- ROI models and spreadsheets. Calven holds customer quotes about return, not a calculator.
- Getting the meeting. That is the champion.
