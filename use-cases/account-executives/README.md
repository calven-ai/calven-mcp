# Account executives

**Use-case pages:** `use-cases/account-executives/`

## Who they are

Account executives own the deal from qualified opportunity to signed contract, and often the renewal and expansion after it. They report to a sales manager or VP Sales and are measured on quota attainment, pipeline coverage, win rate, average deal size, sales cycle length and forecast accuracy. In a B2B SaaS company they work in pods with BDRs who hand them meetings, solutions engineers who carry the technical evaluation, and customer success who takes the account after close. The day is calls, follow-ups and CRM hygiene; the week is a pipeline review and a forecast call; the quarter is a number.

## Responsibilities

**Run the deal**
- Qualify the opportunity: pain, metrics, economic buyer, decision criteria, decision process, champion, competition (MEDDICC or an equivalent)
- Run discovery and map the buying group
- Present the product with the solutions engineer, position it against the alternatives in play
- Build the business case, the proposal and the mutual action plan
- Handle objections on price, product, security, timing and the incumbent
- Negotiate commercial terms with procurement and legal
- Close, then hand the account to customer success with what was promised and why they bought

**Work the territory**
- Build account plans for named accounts: context, stakeholder map, whitespace, next 90 days
- Multi-thread every live deal across champion, economic buyer, technical buyer and end users
- Prospect into the book alongside BDRs; work inbound leads
- Run renewals and expansions in accounts they own

**Report and improve**
- Keep CRM stages, next steps, close dates and amounts true
- Prepare for the weekly pipeline review and the forecast call: what the buyer did this week, the next mutual step, the blocker, who else matters
- Present deals in deal reviews against the qualification framework
- Feed what they hear (objections, gaps, competitor moves) back to product marketing and product

## Recurring activities

| Cadence | Activity |
|---|---|
| Daily | Call prep, discovery and demo calls, follow-up emails and recaps, CRM updates, replies to buyer questions |
| Weekly | Pipeline review with the manager, forecast call, deal reviews on the two or three deals that matter, prospecting block |
| Per deal | Discovery, buying-group mapping, demo with the SE, proposal, exec briefing, negotiation, close, CS handoff |
| Monthly | Account plan refresh on named accounts, renewal pipeline check 90 days out |
| Quarterly | Territory review, QBR with top accounts, lost-deal retrospective, quota reset |

## Where Calven fits

Most of what an AE prepares before a call is knowledge the company already has and nobody has time to find: how similar deals were won, what sank the last four against this competitor, what this buyer's persona cares about, what the product does and does not do. Calven holds that as the approved positioning, battlecards, persona canvases, win/loss evidence, customer quotes and the CRM mirror, and the AE reads it from the AI tool already open in the browser. The call itself, the relationship, the negotiation and the CRM update stay with the rep.
