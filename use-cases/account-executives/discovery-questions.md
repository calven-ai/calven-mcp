# Discovery questions

**Team:** Account executives · also BDRs and SDRs, solutions engineering
**Impact:** High. Discovery sets everything after it: the demo, the business case, the champion, the competitor's room to move.
**Prerequisites:** personas approved, ICP approved. Better with win/loss surveys running (what decides deals), competitors tracked (landmines), call transcripts ingested (how buyers describe the pain).

## What the team is trying to do

Ask the questions that surface the pain, the metric, the buying group, the decision process and the competition, in the order this persona will tolerate, and in words that sound like their world. Done means a short question set per persona and stage that reps actually use, plus the two questions that test the risk that decides deals like this one. Without the company's own knowledge, discovery is a generic checklist and the buyer hears the same questions every vendor asks.

## The work, end to end

| # | Step | What the team does | Where Calven helps | Pulls from |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Know the persona | What they are measured on, what hurts, what they are trying to get done | The persona canvas: goals and KPIs, pains with impact, jobs to be done | Persona canvas |
| 2 | Know the fit | Whether the account is in profile, and which ICP attributes to confirm | The ICP: firmographic, technographic and behavioral attributes; buying triggers; disqualifiers; the account's fit tier if the CRM is connected | ICP document, CRM accounts |
| 3 | Know what decides deals | The forces that decided similar deals, so the questions test them early | Deal drivers by segment and competitor; costly objections | Win/loss dashboard, deal drivers |
| 4 | Know the competitor's weak spot | The landmine question that exposes where the rival loses | Battlecard landmines and discovery questions | Competitor battlecard, deep dive |
| 5 | Write the questions | Pain, metric, economic buyer, decision criteria and process, champion, competition, in the persona's words | A question set per qualification element, phrased from the canvas and the quote library | Persona canvas, quotes, messaging |
| 6 | Sequence them | What to ask first, what to hold for the second call | A sequence tuned to the persona's tolerance and the deal stage | Persona canvas |
| 7 | Run the call and record it | The conversation, the notes, the CRM update | Calven does not help here | |
| 8 | Check what you missed | Which qualification elements the call did not cover | A review of the call notes against the question set and the drivers | Deal drivers, persona canvas |

## Recommended prompts

### Step 1 to 6: the question set for a call

```
Using Calven MCP, write my discovery questions for a first call with a [persona] at [account].

CONTEXT
[Account] is a [segment] company that [how they came in: inbound, referral, outbound]. [Competitor] may be in play. I have 30 minutes and want to leave with pain, a metric, the buying group and the decision process.

PULL FROM THE UNIVERSE
- The [persona] canvas: goals and KPIs, pains, jobs to be done, objections.
- The ICP attributes and buying triggers to confirm for [segment], and the disqualifiers.
- What decided deals in [segment] and against [competitor], from win/loss.
- The landmine questions on the [competitor] battlecard.

BUILD
- Eight to twelve questions grouped by what they establish: pain, metric, economic buyer, decision criteria, decision process, champion, competition. Phrase each the way this persona talks.
- The two questions that test the risk that decided similar deals, marked "ask early".
- The one landmine question and when to ask it.
- The order for a 30-minute call, and what to hold for the next one.

OUTPUT
The question set with a one-line reason per question and a source.

GROUNDING
Ground every question in the canvas, ICP, win/loss drivers or battlecard and cite it. Do not invent pains or triggers the Universe does not record.

[name the persona, account, segment, competitor and how the lead came in]
```

### Step 3: the questions that test the real risk

```
Using Calven MCP, which two questions should I ask early in [deal]?

CONTEXT
[Account], [segment], [persona] champion, [competitor] in play. I want the questions that test what actually sinks deals like this, not a full discovery script.

PULL FROM THE UNIVERSE
- The deal drivers that decided lost deals in [segment] and against [competitor], with the buyers' words.
- The costly objections for this persona.

BUILD
- The two risks most likely to decide this deal, with the evidence.
- One question per risk, phrased for this persona, and who on the buying group to ask.

OUTPUT
Two risks, two questions, sources.

GROUNDING
Rank by how often the driver decided the deal, with n. Do not invent risks the record does not show.

[name the deal, segment, persona and competitor]
```

### Step 8: what I missed

```
Using Calven MCP, review my discovery notes for [deal].

CONTEXT
Below are my notes from the first call with [persona] at [account]. Tell me what I still do not know.

PULL FROM THE UNIVERSE
- The qualification elements we track and what decides deals in [segment].
- The [persona] canvas.

CHECK
- Which of pain, metric, economic buyer, decision criteria, decision process, champion and competition my notes establish, and which they do not.
- Which pain or objection from the canvas never came up, and whether that is a gap or a signal.
- The three questions for the next call.

OUTPUT
A checklist with a verdict per element, then the three questions.

GROUNDING
Judge against the Universe only and cite it. Do not infer answers my notes do not contain.

[paste your notes]
```

### Gap mode: a persona with no discovery evidence

```
Using Calven MCP, how well do we understand [persona] in discovery?

CONTEXT
I keep getting surprised on calls with this persona. I want to know how much evidence we have on them.

PULL FROM THE UNIVERSE
- The [persona] canvas and when it was last updated.
- Quotes from this persona in the last two quarters, by category.
- Win rate on deals where this persona was the contact role, with n.

CHECK
- Which canvas sections have recent quotes behind them and which do not.
- Pains and objections in the quotes that the canvas does not list.

OUTPUT
A short read on where the canvas is strong, where it is thin, and the quotes that should go into the next update.

GROUNDING
Only the canvas, quotes and dashboard figures, cited. If the persona has no quotes, say so.

[name the persona]
```

## Ad hoc questions

- What does a [persona] care about most, and what are they measured on?
- What are the top pains of [persona] in their own words?
- Which ICP attributes should I confirm with a [segment] prospect?
- What disqualifies an account for us?
- What question exposes where [competitor] loses?
- What decided our last ten deals in [segment]?
- How do buyers describe the problem we solve? Give me their phrases.
- What buying triggers show up on accounts we win?
- What does the [persona] object to at the first meeting?
- Which questions did our reps ask on calls that went well?
- What is the economic buyer's persona for a deal like this?
- What does [persona] need to see to become a champion?

## Good practice

- Ask for questions phrased for the persona, not for the framework. The buyer should not hear "what is your decision process".
- Keep the set short. Eight questions asked well beat twenty read off a list.
- Mark the two risk-testing questions and ask them early. The rest can wait.
- Paste your notes after the call and ask what you missed. The second call is cheaper than a late surprise.
- Flag a thin persona canvas to product marketing. Discovery gets better when the persona agent gets the calls.

## Not covered today

- The account's own news, org chart and hiring. The Universe holds the CRM mirror and recorded triggers, not a live company profile.
- Recording and transcribing the call. That happens in the call tool; Calven ingests the result in the app.
- Updating the persona canvas. Gaps go to the persona agent and product marketing in Calven.
