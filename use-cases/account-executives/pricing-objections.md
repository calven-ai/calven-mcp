# Pricing objections

**Team:** Account executives · also sales leadership, finance
**Impact:** Medium. Price is one of the top loss reasons in most CRMs, and the rep's answer usually leans on a discount instead of evidence.
**Prerequisites:** product brief approved (pricing and packaging), win/loss surveys running (pricing and legal survey slice, pricing drivers). Better with competitors tracked (dossier pricing sections) and CRM connected (price feedback on deals).

## What the team is trying to do

Hold price with evidence: what the packaging is, how buyers who paid it describe the value, how often price actually decided a deal versus got named as cover, and where the competitor's price sits as far as the record knows. Done means the rep answers "too expensive" with a value argument and a question, not a discount. Without the company's own evidence, every pricing objection is a negotiation.

## The work, end to end

| # | Step | What the team does | Where Calven helps | Pulls from |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Know the packaging | Plans, what is included, the approved framing | Pricing & Packaging in the product brief | Product brief |
| 2 | Know how price decides deals | Is price a real loss reason or a cover | Pricing drivers with direction and rank; the pricing verdict buckets (cheaper, on par, pricier) and win rate per bucket; loss reason Price on CRM deals | Win/loss dashboard (pricing & legal), competitive intelligence (pricing distribution), CRM deals |
| 3 | Know the competitor's price | Where they sit, as far as recorded | The competitor dossier's pricing and packaging section; pricing signals | Competitor deep dive, competitive signals |
| 4 | Find the value proof | Buyers who paid and describe the return | Quotes tagged quantified outcome or time-to-value | Quotes |
| 5 | Build the response | The answer, the question back, the proof | The approved objection handling for pricing | Messaging document, battlecard |
| 6 | Negotiate | Terms, discounts, approvals | Calven does not help here | |

## Recommended prompts

### Step 1 to 5: answer the pricing objection

```
Using Calven MCP, help me answer a pricing objection in [deal].

CONTEXT
A [persona] at [account] said "[what they said about price]". [Competitor] quoted lower, if that is the case. I want to hold price with evidence.

PULL FROM THE UNIVERSE
- Pricing and packaging from the product brief, and the approved objection handling on price.
- How often price decided deals in [segment]: the pricing drivers with rank, and the win rate when buyers found us cheaper, on par or pricier, with n.
- What the [competitor] dossier says about their pricing, and any recent pricing signal.
- Two quotes from buyers who describe the return, verbatim.

BUILD
- What the buyer is really saying: price, budget, or value not yet shown.
- The answer in the approved framing, then in the customer's words.
- The question back.
- The proof, attributed.
- The honest read: how often this objection actually decides deals like this one.

OUTPUT
A short card with sources.

GROUNDING
Numbers from the dashboards only, with n. Competitor pricing only as the dossier states it; if it says undisclosed, say undisclosed. No discount suggestions.

[paste what the buyer said, name the deal, persona, segment and competitor]
```

### Step 2: does price really decide our deals

```
Using Calven MCP, how often does price actually decide our deals?

CONTEXT
My manager says price is an excuse. I want the record.

PULL FROM THE UNIVERSE
- Deals lost with loss reason Price in [window], and the pricing drivers on surveyed deals: how many ranked as deciding the deal.
- The win rate by pricing verdict (cheaper, on par, pricier) with n.
- The verbatim reasons on deals where price decided it.

BUILD
- The share of losses where price was the stated reason versus where the survey says it decided the deal.
- The pattern in the verbatims: value not shown, budget cycle, or the competitor's number.

OUTPUT
Three numbers with n, then the pattern and the quotes.

GROUNDING
Dashboard and record figures only, cited with window. Do not infer beyond what the sample supports.

[name the window and segment]
```

### Review mode: check my pricing response

## Ad hoc questions

- What is in each plan, as the product brief states it?
- How often is price the reason we lose, and how often did it really decide the deal?
- What is our win rate when the buyer thinks we are pricier?
- What does the [competitor] dossier say they charge?
- Which quotes show the return a customer got?
- What is the approved answer to "it's too expensive"?
- Did [competitor] change pricing recently?
- Which buyers said we were pricier and bought anyway? What did they say?
- What does the ICP say about budget or company size?
- What do buyers on deals lost to price say they needed?

## Good practice

- Separate the objection from the negotiation. Calven answers the first; your manager and the deal desk handle the second.
- Ask for the n. Pricing verdict win rates over a few surveyed deals are a hint.
- Use verbatim return quotes. A customer's number beats a vendor's calculator.
- Never state a price or inclusion beyond the brief. If the brief is silent on a term, say so.

## Not covered today

- Discounts, approvals, terms and deal-desk rules. Not in the Universe.
- Competitor prices beyond what the dossier recorded.
- ROI calculators. Calven holds customer quotes about return, not a model.
