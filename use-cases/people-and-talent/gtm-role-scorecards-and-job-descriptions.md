# GTM role scorecards and job descriptions

**Team:** People and talent · also hiring managers, sales leadership, marketing leadership
**Impact:** High. The scorecard decides who gets hired and the job description decides who applies. Both improve when they name the real segment, buyers and competitors the hire will face instead of "B2B SaaS experience".
**Prerequisites:** strategy documents approved (ICP, positioning, product brief), competitors tracked. Better with personas approved and win/loss surveys running (what the role must be good at to win here).

## What the team is trying to do

Write a scorecard (outcomes, competencies, must-haves) and a job description for an AE, SDR, SE, CSM, PMM or marketer that describe this company's market: the segment and deal size, the personas, the competitors, the sales motion, the category. Done means a scorecard the panel scores against and a job description a candidate in our market recognises as specific. Without the company's own knowledge both are copied from the last posting and the hire is screened for the wrong market.

## The work, end to end

| # | Step | What the team does | Where Calven helps | Pulls from |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Intake | Hiring manager defines outcomes for the first year | Calven does not help here | |
| 2 | Market context | What the role sells, to whom, against whom | ICP segments and deal size bands, personas, Tier 1 competitors, category | ICP, personas, competitors, positioning |
| 3 | Competencies | What matters to win here | Win and loss drivers: which forces decide deals (competitive, capability, experience, commercials); the objections reps must handle | Win/loss dashboard (deciding forces), deal drivers, messaging |
| 4 | Scorecard | Outcomes, competencies, must-haves, weights | A draft with each competency tied to evidence from the Universe | All of the above |
| 5 | Job description | Role, context, responsibilities, requirements, about us | A draft with the market context and the "about us" from positioning and boilerplate | Positioning, messaging (boilerplate), ICP |
| 6 | Review | Hiring manager and recruiter align | A check that the description matches the approved story | Positioning, messaging |
| 7 | Post and source | Publish, outreach | Calven does not help here | |

## Recommended prompts

### Steps 2 to 4: the scorecard

```
Using Calven MCP, draft the scorecard for a new [role].

CONTEXT
The hiring manager's first-year outcomes are at the bottom. I want competencies and must-haves grounded in our actual market and in what decides our deals.

PULL FROM THE UNIVERSE
- The ICP: segment tiers, deal size bands if recorded, priority verticals.
- The personas this role works with: role titles and seniority.
- Tier 1 competitors and our win rate against each.
- What kind of force decides our deals, and the top win and loss drivers with a verbatim each.
- The objections in our messaging this role must handle.

BUILD
- Outcomes (from the manager, restated).
- Competencies: five to seven, each with the evidence from our deals for why it matters here and a one-line "what good looks like".
- Must-haves: the segment, buyer and competitive experience that our market requires.
- Nice-to-haves.

OUTPUT
The scorecard as a table with sources per competency.

GROUNDING
Tie each competency to the Universe and cite it. Do not add requirements our market does not support.

[paste the first-year outcomes and name the role]
```

### Step 5: the job description

```
Using Calven MCP, write the job description for [role].

CONTEXT
Candidates in our market should read it and recognise the job: the segment, the buyers, the competitors, the motion. No generic SaaS language. Under 600 words.

PULL FROM THE UNIVERSE
- Our positioning statement and category, and the messaging boilerplate for the "about us".
- The ICP: who we sell to, segment, verticals.
- The personas this role talks to.
- Tier 1 competitors, named or described as the category alternatives.
- The scorecard competencies from the previous step.

WRITE
- About us: three sentences from positioning and boilerplate.
- The role: what you sell or support, to whom, against whom, with what motion.
- Responsibilities: six bullets.
- What we look for: from the scorecard.
- What you get: [the team pastes compensation and benefits].

OUTPUT
The job description, with a note on which lines came from positioning and ICP.

GROUNDING
Use only positioning, messaging, ICP and personas in the Universe and cite them. Do not claim traction, funding or customer names unless the boilerplate carries them.

[name the role and paste the scorecard]
```

### Step 6: review

```
Using Calven MCP, check this job description against our approved story.

CONTEXT
Below is the draft. I want anything off-category, off-message or inconsistent with the ICP flagged.

PULL FROM THE UNIVERSE
- Positioning, messaging boilerplate, ICP.

CHECK
- Flag lines that describe a different market, buyer or category from the approved documents.
- Flag claims the Universe does not support.
- Suggest the on-message line.

OUTPUT
The draft annotated, then a clean version.

GROUNDING
Judge only against the Universe and cite each flag. If it is consistent, say so.

[paste the draft]
```

## Ad hoc questions

- Which segment and deal size will a new AE work, per the ICP?
- Which personas does an SDR here talk to?
- Which competitors will the hire sell against most?
- What decides our deals: product, experience, price or competition?
- What is our one-paragraph "about us" from the boilerplate?
- Which objections must a new CSM be able to handle?
- What category do we claim, so the posting uses the right words?
- What experience would help a PMM here, given our personas and competitors?
- What are our priority verticals, for the requirements section?

## Good practice

- Start from outcomes the manager owns; Calven supplies the market context, not the outcomes.
- Name the competitors in the scorecard and describe them as alternatives in the public posting if naming them is not wanted.
- Tie every competency to a deal driver. "Handles competitive deals" means something when the competitive force decides 40 percent of deals.
- Reuse the "about us" paragraph across postings; it comes from the boilerplate, so it is the same everywhere.

## Not covered today

- Compensation, levels and benefits.
- The ATS posting, sourcing and outreach.
- Legal requirements in the posting.
