# Candidate screening briefs

**Team:** People and talent · also hiring managers, founders hiring their first GTM roles
**Impact:** High. The screen is where a recruiter or founder judges whether a candidate understands the buyer, the market and the competition. Most screeners do not know those well themselves. A brief pulled from the approved Universe makes the screen a test of the candidate, not of the interviewer.
**Prerequisites:** strategy documents approved (ICP, positioning, product brief), personas approved, competitors tracked. Better with win/loss surveys running (what wins deals) and call transcripts ingested (how buyers talk).

## What the team is trying to do

Walk into a screen for an AE, SDR, CSM, SE, PMM or marketer knowing what good looks like for this company: the segment and buyers the hire will work, the competitors they will meet, the objections they will hear, the way deals are won. Done means a two-page brief per role, three screening questions with model answers, and a way to tell a generic answer from one that fits this market. Without the company's own knowledge the screener asks "tell me about a deal you closed" and cannot judge the answer.

## The work, end to end

| # | Step | What the team does | Where Calven helps | Pulls from |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Role intake | Agree with the hiring manager what the role sells, to whom, against whom | ICP segments, personas, competitors as the shared vocabulary | ICP, personas, competitors |
| 2 | Market brief | The screener learns the market and category | Positioning statement, category and alternatives; High trends | Positioning, trends |

| 8 | Debrief | Compare answers with the model, pass or decline | The brief as the reference | All of the above |

## Recommended prompts

### Steps 2 to 5: the screening brief

```
Using Calven MCP, brief me before I screen candidates for [role].

CONTEXT
I am screening [number] candidates for [role] this week. I need to understand our market, buyers and competitors well enough to tell a candidate who gets it from one who recites a generic SaaS answer.

PULL FROM THE UNIVERSE
- Our positioning: category, frame of reference, competitive alternatives, unique attributes.
- The ICP: segment tiers, priority verticals, disqualifiers.
- The buyer personas this role works with: role title, top pains, top objections, messaging hooks.
- Tier 1 competitors: one line each, where we win, where we lose, our win rate against each with n.
- The top three win drivers with a buyer verbatim each.

BUILD
- A two-page brief in five sections: market, ICP, buyers, competitors, how we win.
- For each section, the one thing a strong candidate would say unprompted and the generic line a weak candidate would say instead.

OUTPUT
The brief with sources, and the "strong versus generic" list.

GROUNDING
Use only the approved documents, records and dashboards in the Universe and cite them. Do not invent competitor facts or buyer behaviour.

[name the role and the segment it will work]
```

### Step 6: screening questions

```
Using Calven MCP, write screening questions for [role] with model answers.

CONTEXT
A 30-minute screen. I want five questions that test whether the candidate understands buyers like ours and selling against competitors like ours, each with a model answer from our Universe and a note on what a generic answer sounds like.

PULL FROM THE UNIVERSE
- The [persona] canvas: pains, objections, KPIs.
- The battlecard for [competitor]: where we win, where we lose, landmines, objection handling.
- The ICP's buying triggers and disqualifiers.

BUILD
- Five questions: one on the persona's pains, one on handling the persona's top objection, one on selling against [competitor], one on qualifying an account against our ICP, one on the category.
- For each: the model answer (what someone who knows this market says), the generic answer, and a 1 to 4 score guide.

OUTPUT
The five questions with answers and the score guide.

GROUNDING
Model answers come only from the Universe, cited. Do not expect the candidate to know our internal data; the test is reasoning about buyers and rivals like ours.

[name the role, persona and competitor]
```

### Step 8: debrief against the brief

```
Using Calven MCP, help me judge this candidate's answers.

CONTEXT
Below are my notes from the screen for [role]. I want each answer compared with what our Universe says about the buyer, the competitor and the ICP.

PULL FROM THE UNIVERSE
- The [persona] canvas, the [competitor] battlecard, the ICP.

CHECK
- For each answer: does it match how the persona actually behaves and what the battlecard says, or is it generic or wrong for our market.
- One line per answer: strong, acceptable, off.

OUTPUT
A scored table and a recommendation to advance or decline, with the reason.

GROUNDING
Judge only against the Universe and cite it. Do not penalise a candidate for not knowing internal facts; judge the reasoning.

[paste your notes]
```

## Ad hoc questions

- Brief me on our ICP and competitors before I screen AE candidates.
- Who is the persona an SDR here calls most, and what is their top objection?
- What does a good answer to "how would you sell against [competitor]" sound like for us?
- Where do we lose to [competitor]? I want to hear if the candidate can guess it.
- What are our ICP disqualifiers? I want to test qualification.
- Which three questions do our reps ask in discovery, per the battlecards?
- How do our best reps phrase our value, from the call quotes?
- What is our category, in our words?
- What wins deals here, per win/loss?
- Which buying triggers should a BDR candidate recognise?
- What is the one thing a PMM candidate should notice about our messaging?

## Good practice

- Brief yourself per role, not per company. The AE brief is about competitors; the CSM brief is about user personas and renewals.
- Test reasoning, not recall. Give the candidate the persona or the competitor in the question and judge what they do with it.
- Keep the "strong versus generic" list beside you. It is the fastest way to score a screen.
- Reuse the brief for the hiring manager and the panel so everyone scores against the same picture.
- Refresh the brief per requisition; personas and battlecards move.

## Not covered today

- ATS data, candidate pipeline and scheduling.
- Compensation benchmarks.
- Reference and background checks.
- The candidate's own history; the brief is about our market, not their CV.
