# Campaign briefs


A campaign needs the document it runs on: goal, audience, the one idea, messages by persona and stage, proof, channels and assets, and what not to say. You walk away with a brief any writer can produce on-message copy from without a call with the PMM. Calven saves you assembling it from the last deck and a persona slide for every campaign.

## Prompts

### Write the campaign brief

```
Using Calven MCP, write the campaign brief for the campaign below.

FILL IN
- Campaign: [campaign]
- Goal: [pipeline or lead goal]
- Personas: [personas]
- Segment: [segment]
- Dates: [dates]
- Channels: [channels]

CONTEXT
Audience: the personas in the segment. Runs on the dates across the channels. Writers, designers and the agency will work from this without talking to me.

PULL FROM THE UNIVERSE
- The ICP's read on the segment: pains, use cases, buying triggers, disqualifiers.
- The persona canvases for the personas and the messaging matrix for them by stage.
- The top pains, buying triggers and objections customers in this segment raised, with verbatim quotes.
- Proof points and customer quotes that support the campaign's message.
- The product brief entries for anything the campaign mentions.
- Where the personas learn, from the canvases.

BUILD
- The one idea everything ladders up to.
- Audience: who, what they are measured on, the live pain, in their words.
- A persona by stage message table: what we say, the proof, the CTA.
- Objections to pre-empt and the approved response.
- Language guide: ten phrases to use (from quotes), five to avoid.
- Claim boundaries: what the brief allows and what it does not.
- Channels and assets with the message each carries.

OUTPUT
A campaign brief document with sources, plus a one-page summary for the agency.

GROUNDING
Ground every message, quote and claim in the Universe and cite it. Do not invent personas, stages or proof. Flag any gap the team must fill.
```

### Find the strongest campaign angle

```
Using Calven MCP, find the strongest angle for a campaign aimed at the persona in the segment below.

FILL IN
- Persona: [persona]
- Segment: [segment]
- Window: [time window, e.g. last quarter]

PULL FROM THE UNIVERSE
- Themes with the most mentions for this persona in the window, by category (pain, trigger, job).
- Buying triggers and costly objections from the voice-of-customer read.
- Trends relevant to the segment with high severity.

BUILD
Three candidate angles: the pain or trigger, how often it appears, the quote that proves it, the trend that makes it timely, and the pillar it leads to. Recommend one.

OUTPUT
The three angles and the recommendation.

GROUNDING
Counts from the dashboards with n. Quotes verbatim. Do not invent an angle the evidence does not support.
```

### Set the competitive frame

```
Using Calven MCP, set the competitive frame for a campaign aimed at the customers of the competitor below.

FILL IN
- Competitor: [competitor]

PULL FROM THE UNIVERSE
- The competitor's battlecard: where we win, landmines, objection handling.
- Why buyers switched from them, from won-deal drivers and quotes.

BUILD
- The three switching reasons, in the buyer's words.
- The two landmines a campaign can set without naming the rival.
- What not to claim.

OUTPUT
A half-page frame for the brief.

GROUNDING
Only the battlecard and deal evidence, cited.
```

### Check an agency brief against the story

```
Using Calven MCP, review this agency brief against our approved story.

FILL IN
- Brief: [paste the brief]

PULL FROM THE UNIVERSE
- Our ICP, the persona canvases named, the messaging matrix, the product brief.

CHECK
- Audience description vs the ICP and canvases.
- Messages vs the matrix; claims vs the brief.
- Language vs how customers talk.

OUTPUT
The brief annotated, then the corrected version.

GROUNDING
Judge only against the Universe and cite each flag.
```

## Advanced prompts

### Simulate the campaigns before you fund one

```
Simulate the campaigns I'm choosing between and tell me which one to fund. Use Calven MCP for the audience, the buyers' reactions and our real conversion history.

FILL IN
- Campaign options: [paste two to four concepts: angle, offer, channels, budget]
- Funnel benchmarks: [paste last year's channel results: spend, CTR, conversion by stage, or write "none"]
- Pipeline target: [target for the quarter]
- Segment: [segment]

CONTEXT
I have one quarter's budget and room for one bet. Before I spend it, I want each option played forward: how many of the right accounts it can reach, how the buyers react to it, and what pipeline it plausibly produces.

FROM CALVEN
- The ICP for the segment and the count of Tier 1 and Tier 2 accounts in the CRM there, with how many already have an open deal.
- A persona review of each concept's headline and offer, run against the personas the campaign targets.
- Win rate, average deal size and sales cycle for the segment from the ICP dashboard, with n.

SIMULATE
- For each option, build a funnel from reachable accounts to closed revenue. Use my benchmarks where I gave them and a stated assumption where I didn't.
- Adjust each stage by the persona review: a concept the buyer pushes back on converts worse. Say how much you discounted and why.
- Run it as a range, not a point: low, expected and high for MQLs, opportunities, pipeline and revenue. If you can run code, run a Monte Carlo with 1,000 draws and show the distribution.
- Show the three assumptions that move the result most, and how far each would have to move to change the winner.

OUTPUT
A scorecard, one row per option: reachable accounts, persona verdict, MQLs, opportunities, pipeline, revenue, cost per opportunity, confidence. Then the recommendation in three lines and the two-week test that would de-risk it.

GROUNDING
Label every number as Calven (cited, with n), mine, or your assumption. Don't invent a benchmark I didn't give you; use a labelled range. Persona reactions come from the review, not your guess.
```

### Run a pre-mortem on the campaign

```
Run a pre-mortem on my campaign: assume it's ninety days from launch and it failed, and tell me why. Use Calven MCP for the objections, the competitor's likely response and what has sunk similar deals.

FILL IN
- Campaign brief: [paste the brief]
- Segment: [segment]
- Competitor: [main competitor in this segment]

CONTEXT
The brief looks good to everyone who wrote it, which is the problem. I want the failure stories while there's still time to change the plan.

FROM CALVEN
- The objections the target personas raise, from their canvases and from call quotes tagged Objection.
- The competitor's battlecard and their signals from the last 90 days.
- The top loss reasons for the segment from the win/loss dashboard, with n.

METHOD
- Write five short post-mortems, each from a different cause: wrong audience, message that doesn't land, competitor counter-move, sales follow-up that drops the ball, offer the buyer doesn't want.
- Rate each for likelihood and damage, grounded in the evidence above.
- For the top two, name the early signal I'd see in the first two weeks and the change to the brief that prevents it.

OUTPUT
A table of the five failure modes with likelihood, damage, early signal and fix, then the brief with the two fixes marked.

GROUNDING
Every failure cause cites Calven evidence or is labelled as your judgement. Don't invent a competitor move nobody recorded.
```

### Put the brief in front of a synthetic buying committee

```
Put my campaign in front of a synthetic buying committee and tell me where the deal stalls. Use Calven MCP to build each member from our approved personas and real buyer quotes.

FILL IN
- Campaign assets: [paste the email, landing page copy and offer]
- Segment: [segment]

CONTEXT
A campaign rarely wins over one person. I want to see how the champion, the economic buyer, the technical buyer and the likely blocker each react, and whether the champion can sell it internally.

FROM CALVEN
- The buyer, stakeholder and user personas with their canvases: goals, pains, objections.
- Verbatim quotes from those roles in the segment.
- A persona review of the assets.

METHOD
- Play each committee member reading the assets cold, in the first person, in their own words.
- Then run the internal meeting: the champion pitches the idea to the others, each pushes back, and the meeting ends with a decision.
- Score the campaign on how far it gets the committee: interest, a meeting, a shortlist, or nothing.

OUTPUT
The four reactions, the meeting transcript in under 400 words, the stall point, and the two lines of copy that would get past it.

GROUNDING
Every reaction traces to a persona canvas, a quote or the review, cited. Mark anything beyond that as your extrapolation.
```

## Ad hoc questions

- What is the top pain for [persona] in [segment] this quarter, with a quote?
- Which buying triggers appear most in [segment]?
- What is our value proposition for [persona] at the consideration stage?
- Give me three proof points for [pillar].
- Which objections should a [segment] campaign pre-empt?
- What phrases do customers use for [topic]?
- Where does [persona] learn: which events, communities, publications?
- Is [segment] Tier 1 in our ICP?
- What are the disqualifiers for [segment]?
- Which claim can I make about [capability] in an ad?
- What is the one-liner for [product]?
- What trend makes [topic] timely for [segment]?
- Which segment has the highest win rate but the least open pipeline, with n?
- Which pain shows up in won-deal drivers but not in any of our messaging pillars?
- Which of [persona]'s objections have no approved response in the messaging?
- What do customers in [segment] call the problem before they know our category exists?
- Which competitor signals from the last 90 days should change a [segment] campaign's angle?
- Which of our proof points come from customers in [segment] specifically?
