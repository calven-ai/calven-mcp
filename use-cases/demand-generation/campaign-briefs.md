# Campaign briefs

**Team:** Demand generation · also product marketing, content marketing, agencies
**Impact:** High. Every writer, designer and agency on a campaign works from the brief; one built from the approved ICP, personas, messaging and proof keeps a dozen assets telling one story.
**Prerequisites:** strategy documents approved (ICP, messaging, positioning, product brief), personas approved. Better with transcripts ingested (customer language, quotes), CRM connected (segment data), win/loss (objections, proof).

## What the team is trying to do

Write the document a campaign runs on: goal, audience, the one idea, messages by persona and stage, proof, channels and assets, what not to say. Done means a brief any writer can produce on-message copy from without a call with the PMM. Without the Universe the brief is assembled from the last deck, a persona slide and a call with the PMM for every campaign.

## Recommended prompts

### Step 1 to 9: the brief

```
Using Calven MCP, write the campaign brief for [campaign].

CONTEXT
Goal: [pipeline or lead goal]. Audience: [personas] in [segment]. Runs [dates] across [channels]. Writers, designers and the agency will work from this without talking to me.

PULL FROM THE UNIVERSE
- The ICP's read on [segment]: pains, use cases, buying triggers, disqualifiers.
- The persona canvases for [personas] and the messaging matrix for them by stage.
- The top pains, buying triggers and objections customers in this segment raised, with verbatim quotes.
- Proof points and customer quotes that support the campaign's message.
- The product brief entries for anything the campaign mentions.
- Where [personas] learn, from the canvases.

BUILD
- The one idea everything ladders up to.
- Audience: who, what they are measured on, the live pain, in their words.
- A persona by stage message table: what we say, the proof, the CTA.
- Objections to pre-empt and the approved response.
- Language guide: ten phrases to use (from quotes), five to avoid.
- Claim boundaries: what the brief allows and what it does not.
- Channels and assets with the message each carries.

OUTPUT
A campaign brief document with sources, plus a one-page summary for the agency.

GROUNDING
Ground every message, quote and claim in the Universe and cite it. Do not invent personas, stages or proof. Flag any gap the team must fill.

[name the campaign, goal, personas, segment, dates and channels]
```

### Step 2: find the angle

```
Using Calven MCP, find the strongest angle for a campaign aimed at [persona] in [segment].

PULL FROM THE UNIVERSE
- Themes with the most mentions for this persona in [window], by category (pain, trigger, job).
- Buying triggers and costly objections from the voice-of-customer read.
- Trends relevant to [segment] with high severity.

BUILD
Three candidate angles: the pain or trigger, how often it appears, the quote that proves it, the trend that makes it timely, and the pillar it leads to. Recommend one.

OUTPUT
The three angles and the recommendation.

GROUNDING
Counts from the dashboards with n. Quotes verbatim. Do not invent an angle the evidence does not support.

[name the persona, segment and window]
```

### Step 6: competitive campaign frame

```
Using Calven MCP, set the competitive frame for a campaign aimed at [competitor]'s customers.

PULL FROM THE UNIVERSE
- The battlecard for [competitor]: where we win, landmines, objection handling.
- Why buyers switched from them, from won-deal drivers and quotes.

BUILD
- The three switching reasons, in the buyer's words.
- The two landmines a campaign can set without naming the rival.
- What not to claim.

OUTPUT
A half-page frame for the brief.

GROUNDING
Only the battlecard and deal evidence, cited.

[name the competitor]
```

### Review mode: check an agency brief

```
Using Calven MCP, review this agency brief against our approved story.

PULL FROM THE UNIVERSE
- Our ICP, the persona canvases named, the messaging matrix, the product brief.

CHECK
- Audience description vs the ICP and canvases.
- Messages vs the matrix; claims vs the brief.
- Language vs how customers talk.

OUTPUT
The brief annotated, then the corrected version.

GROUNDING
Judge only against the Universe and cite each flag.

[paste the brief]
```

## Ad hoc questions

- What is the top pain for [persona] in [segment] this quarter, with a quote?
- Which buying triggers appear most in [segment]?
- What is our value proposition for [persona] at the consideration stage?
- Give me three proof points for [pillar].
- Which objections should a [segment] campaign pre-empt?
- What phrases do customers use for [topic]?
- Where does [persona] learn: which events, communities, publications?
- Is [segment] Tier 1 in our ICP?
- What are the disqualifiers for [segment]?
- Which claim can I make about [capability] in an ad?
- What is the one-liner for [product]?
- What trend makes [topic] timely for [segment]?

## Good practice

- Write the brief before any asset. Every review later cites it.
- Name the personas and segment as Calven names them.
- Ask for the language guide from quotes. It is the part writers use most.
- Include claim boundaries. The agency does not know what the product does not do.
- Rebuild the brief, not the assets, when the messaging changes.

## Not covered today

- Budget, media plan and targeting setup in the ad platforms.
- Creative direction and design.
- Performance data; that returns in the campaign retro.
