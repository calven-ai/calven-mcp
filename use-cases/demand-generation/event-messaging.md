# Event messaging

**Team:** Demand generation · also product marketing, business development, account executives
**Impact:** Medium. A conference compresses the whole funnel into two days; booth copy, the talk, the outreach before and after, and the on-floor talk track all need the same story tuned to the attendees' personas.
**Prerequisites:** personas approved, messaging approved, positioning approved. Better with competitors tracked (rivals on the floor), CRM connected (attending accounts), transcripts (language).

## What the team is trying to do

Prepare everything the team says at an event: the booth line, the talk abstract, pre-event outreach, the floor conversation, the competitive answers, and the follow-up. Done means an event messaging kit by persona and a target list for outreach. Without the Universe the booth says the homepage and the reps improvise.

## Recommended prompts

### Step 1 to 3 and 6: the event kit

```
Using Calven MCP, build the messaging kit for [event].

CONTEXT
Attendees are mostly [personas]. We have a booth and [a talk slot / none]. Rivals exhibiting: [competitors].

PULL FROM THE UNIVERSE
- The persona canvases for [personas]: pains, hooks, objections.
- Our positioning and the messaging hooks and one-liner.
- Themes and trends relevant to this audience, with quotes.
- The battlecards for [competitors].

BUILD
- The booth line and two signage lines, in the attendees' words.
- A talk title and 100-word abstract built on a pain and a trend.
- The 30-second floor pitch per persona, three discovery questions, the three objections and responses.
- The competitive answers: "we use [competitor]" for each rival on the floor.

OUTPUT
The kit by section, with sources.

GROUNDING
Language from quotes, claims from the brief, competitive lines from the battlecards, cited.

[name the event, personas, competitors and whether we have a talk]
```

### Step 4 and 5: outreach list and messages

```
Using Calven MCP, build the pre-event outreach for [event].

CONTEXT
Below is the attendee or sponsor list we have.

PULL FROM THE UNIVERSE
- Which of these companies are CRM accounts, with fit tier, triggers and any open or lost deal.
- Contacts we know at them by role.
- The persona messages for the roles we will meet.

BUILD
- The accounts ranked by fit with the reason and the history.
- A meeting-request note per persona, 60 words, opening on their pain.

OUTPUT
The ranked list and the notes.

GROUNDING
Only the CRM mirror and canvases, cited. Names follow the workspace security settings.

[paste the attendee list and name the event]
```

### Step 9: follow-up

```
Using Calven MCP, write the post-event follow-ups for [event].

PULL FROM THE UNIVERSE
- The messaging for the consideration stage for [personas].
- Proof points and quotes on the topics that came up.

WRITE
Three follow-ups: met at the booth, attended the talk, requested a meeting, each with the next step.

GROUNDING
Claims from the brief, quotes verbatim, cited.

[name the personas and the topics that came up]
```

## Ad hoc questions

- Which personas attend [event type] per our canvases?
- What is our one-liner for a booth banner?
- Which attending companies are Tier 1 accounts?
- How do we answer "we already use [competitor]" on the floor?
- What discovery questions work for [persona]?
- Give me a 30-second pitch for [persona].
- Which trend would make a good talk for [audience]?
- Which of the attendees have an open deal with us?

## Good practice

- Build the kit by persona; a booth hears five roles a day.
- Rank attendees by fit before outreach. Meetings with Tier 3 accounts fill the calendar and nothing else.
- Keep the competitive lines short and honest; rivals are two booths away.
- Follow up by the conversation that happened, not by list.

## Not covered today

- The attendee list itself. The organiser provides it; the AI tool matches it to the CRM mirror.
- Booking meetings, booth logistics, the talk slides.
