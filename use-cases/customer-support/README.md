# Customer support

**Use-case pages:** `use-cases/customer-support/`

## Who they are

Support agents (tier 1 and tier 2), technical support engineers, a support manager or head of support, and in larger teams a support operations or QA lead. They report to a VP of Customer Experience, the head of customer success, or operations. Measured on first response time, resolution time, first-contact resolution, CSAT, SLA compliance and ticket deflection. They answer product questions all day and are often the first to hear a customer name a competitor.

## Responsibilities

**Answering and resolving**
- Triage incoming tickets, reproduce problems, resolve what is known, escalate what is not.
- Answer "does it do X", "how does X work", "what does plan Y include" questions accurately and in the company's approved words.
- Explain integrations, limits and architecture to technical users.

**Escalation**
- Hand complex or high-impact cases to tier 2 or engineering with reproduction steps, logs and the customer's own words.
- Flag at-risk accounts to customer success and sales: repeated unresolved issues, frustration, a competitor named in a ticket.

**Knowledge**
- Write and maintain help-centre articles, FAQs, macros and internal runbooks.
- Keep answers current when the product changes, a feature is deprecated or pricing moves.

**Voice of the customer**
- Tag and categorise feedback, send a weekly report of top issues and requests to product.
- Spot the pattern across tickets rather than the loudest voice.

**Team**
- Onboard and train new agents on the product, the customers and the competitors.
- Run QA on a sample of tickets each week against a rubric.

## Recurring activities

| Cadence | Activity |
|---|---|
| Daily | Ticket triage, answering, escalation, macro use |
| Weekly | Feedback report to product, QA sample review, help-centre updates, escalation review with CS |
| Monthly | CSAT and KPI review, knowledge-base gap audit, agent coaching |
| Per release | Update macros and articles, brief the team on what changed, handle the spike in "what happened to X" tickets |
| Per new hire | Product, customer and competitor onboarding, shadowing, certification |

## Where Calven fits

Support answers from the product and the customer, so the facts they need are the approved product brief, the pricing and packaging, the competitor battlecards and what other customers say about the same problem. Calven holds all of those and keeps them current, which is what a macro written last year does not. The ticket system, product usage data and the help centre itself are not in Calven; Calven supplies the approved facts and context an agent puts into the answer.
