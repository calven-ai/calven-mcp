# Business development (BDRs and SDRs)


## Who they are

Business development representatives (outbound) and sales development representatives (inbound) sit at the top of the funnel. They report to a sales development manager or the VP of Sales, sometimes to marketing. They are measured on qualified meetings booked, opportunities accepted by AEs, pipeline sourced, and activity (touches, calls, replies). An outbound BDR's day is research, list building, sequencing and personalisation; an inbound SDR's day is speed to lead, qualification calls and follow-up. Both work from a persona slide, a battlecard and whatever the last rep told them, so the quality of their outreach is capped by how current and specific that knowledge is.

## Responsibilities

**Pipeline generation**
- Build and refresh target account lists against the ICP; map the buying committee at each account
- Research accounts and contacts before the first touch
- Run multi-channel sequences (email, phone, LinkedIn, voicemail) of four to seven touches over two to three weeks
- Personalise by segment (industry, role, size) and by account (trigger, pain, named proof)
- Follow up on buying signals: funding, exec hires, M&A, tech migrations, expansion, restructuring

**Inbound response and qualification**
- Respond to inbound leads inside minutes, not hours
- Qualify against the ICP and a framework (BANT, MEDDIC fields the AE needs)
- Route and hand off with notes the AE can act on: pain, people, timeline, competitor in play

**Conversations**
- Cold calls from a talk track: opener, reason for the call, value, open question
- Handle brush-offs and objections ("we already use X", "no budget", "send me an email", "not a priority")
- Leave voicemails that earn a callback; write LinkedIn messages that get accepted and answered
- Follow up after events and webinars; close out sequences with a breakup email

**Feedback loop**
- Log objections and replies by persona and industry; report which messages and verticals land
- Keep the CRM clean: contacts, roles, lifecycle stage, activity
- Feed marketing and PMM what the market says back

## Recurring activities

| Cadence | Activity |
|---|---|
| Daily | Work overnight inbound before it goes cold; 40 to 60 dials or 30 to 50 personalised emails; sequence steps due today; CRM updates |
| Weekly | Build next week's account and contact list; review sequence reply and meeting rates; team stand-up on what is landing; one-on-one with the manager on pipeline and skills |
| Monthly | Refresh objection responses; retire weak sequence steps; review which segments convert to accepted opportunities |
| Per trigger | A buying signal fires on an account: research, angle, outreach within days |
| Per event | Pre-event outreach to registrants; post-event follow-up within 48 hours |
| Quarterly | New territory or segment assignment; sequence rebuild on new messaging; win-back pass on closed-lost accounts |

## Where Calven fits

Nearly everything a BDR writes depends on three things the company already knows and rarely gives them in usable form: who the persona is and what they say on calls, why similar accounts bought or walked, and what the competitor in the inbox is weak at. Calven MCP puts the persona canvas, the customer quotes, the deal drivers, the battlecards and the CRM mirror with ICP fit behind the BDR's own AI tool, so a draft is tested on the buyer before it is sent and an objection is answered from deals the company won. Calven does not watch the prospect's news, read email engagement or write back to the CRM; the sequencer and the CRM stay where they are.
