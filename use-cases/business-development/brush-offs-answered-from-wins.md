# Brush-offs answered from wins

**Team:** Business development · also account executives, sales enablement
**Impact:** High. "We already use [competitor]" and "not a priority" end most cold conversations; an answer drawn from buyers who said the same thing and then bought keeps the conversation alive.
**Prerequisites:** competitors tracked (battlecards), win/loss surveys running or call transcripts ingested (deal drivers, quotes). Messaging approved for the objection handling section.

## What the team is trying to do

Answer a brush-off in the moment with something true and specific: what changed the mind of a buyer who said the same thing. Done means a two-line response per brush-off per persona that the BDR knows by heart, plus the evidence behind it for the follow-up email. Without the company's own deals the BDR argues features against an incumbent the prospect is happy with.

## The work, end to end

| # | Step | What the team does | Where Calven helps | Pulls from |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Collect the brush-offs | List what prospects actually say on calls and in replies | The objections customers raise most, ranked, with verbatim quotes | Voice-of-customer dashboard (objections), quotes (category Objection) |
| 2 | Find who said it and bought | For each brush-off, the won deals where it came up | Deal drivers on won deals where the objection appeared and what outweighed it | Deal drivers, surveyed deals |
| 3 | Pull the competitor answer | For "we already use X", what the battlecard says | Objection handling ("They say, you say"), where we win, landmines | Competitor battlecard |
| 4 | Write the response | Two lines to say, one question to ask | A response in the buyer's words, grounded in the won deal | Quotes, deal drivers, messaging (objection handling) |
| 5 | Check it | Make sure it claims nothing we cannot back | The claim against the product brief and the approved objection handling | Product brief, messaging |
| 6 | Write the follow-up | The email after the call that carries the proof | The won-deal story and the quote | Surveyed deals, quotes |
| 7 | Practise | Rehearse before the next block of calls | Role-play as the persona raising the brush-off | Persona canvas |

## Recommended prompts

### Steps 1 and 2: what buyers who bought said at first

```
Using Calven MCP, show me the brush-offs [persona]s give us and what changed their minds.

CONTEXT
I am building my responses for cold calls to [persona] in [segment].

PULL FROM THE UNIVERSE
- The objections this persona raises most, from the voice-of-customer read, with a verbatim quote each.
- For each objection, a won deal where it came up and the driver that outweighed it, with the buyer's words.

BUILD
- A table: brush-off · how often it came up · the won deal where it was overcome · what outweighed it · the quote.

OUTPUT
The table, ranked by how often the brush-off appears.

GROUNDING
Use only objections, drivers and quotes from the Universe and cite them. If no won deal shows an objection being overcome, say so rather than inventing one.

[name the persona and segment]
```

### Steps 3 and 4: "we already use [competitor]"

```
Using Calven MCP, give me my answer to "we already use [competitor]".

CONTEXT
A [persona] at a [segment] account just told me this on a call. I have fifteen seconds.

PULL FROM THE UNIVERSE
- The battlecard for [competitor]: objection handling, where we win, the landmines.
- Deals we won against [competitor] and the driver that decided them, with the buyer's words.
- Why we lost to [competitor], so I do not walk into it.

BUILD
- Two lines to say, in plain speech, that acknowledge the incumbent and open one question the battlecard says exposes their weakness.
- The proof for the follow-up email: the won deal and the quote.
- The one thing not to claim.

OUTPUT
The two lines, the question, the follow-up proof, the thing not to say.

GROUNDING
Use only the battlecard and win/loss evidence in the Universe and cite it. Do not invent competitor weaknesses or customer outcomes.

[name the competitor and the persona]
```

### Step 4: the other brush-offs

```
Using Calven MCP, write my responses to these brush-offs for [persona].

CONTEXT
The brush-offs I hear most are at the bottom. For each I want two lines to say and the evidence behind them.

PULL FROM THE UNIVERSE
- Our approved objection handling from the messaging document.
- The customer quotes and won-deal drivers that back each response.

WRITE
- For each brush-off: acknowledge in one line, reframe with a question in one line, and the proof to send after.

OUTPUT
A response card per brush-off, with sources.

GROUNDING
Use only the objection handling, quotes and drivers in the Universe and cite them. Where there is no evidence for a response, say so and give the question to ask instead.

[list the brush-offs]
```

### Step 7: practise

```
Using Calven MCP, play [persona] and brush me off.

CONTEXT
I want to rehearse the three brush-offs I hear most before my call block. The setup is at the bottom.

PULL FROM THE UNIVERSE
- The [persona] canvas: objections, how they talk, what they find credible.

ROLE-PLAY
- Open as the persona would when a cold call lands. Brush me off the way the canvas says you would. Respond to what I say in character. After three exchanges, drop character and tell me what worked and the line I should drop.

OUTPUT
An interactive exchange, then a two-line debrief.

GROUNDING
Stay true to the persona as defined in the Universe. Do not make the buyer easier than the canvas says.

[name the persona and the product; say which brush-off to start with]
```

## Ad hoc questions

- How do we answer "we already use [competitor]"?
- What outweighed price in deals we won against [competitor]?
- Which brush-off do [persona]s give most often?
- What did a buyer who first said "not a priority" say after they bought?
- What is the landmine question for [competitor]?
- Why do we lose to [competitor], in one line?
- Give me a quote from a customer who switched from [competitor].
- What does our messaging say to "we built this in-house"?
- Which objection costs us the most deals right now?
- What does [persona] find credible as proof?
- Is there a won deal in [industry] where "no budget" came up?
- What should I never claim when [competitor] is the incumbent?

## Good practice

- Ask for two lines, not a paragraph. The response has to survive a live call.
- Ask for the won deal behind the response. A response with no deal behind it is an opinion.
- Separate "what to say" from "what to send". The quote and the deal story belong in the follow-up email, not on the call.
- Check the loss reasons for the same competitor. The best answer to a brush-off avoids the trap we usually fall into.
- Refresh the card when the battlecard changes. Ask what changed for [competitor] in the last month.

## Not covered today

- Call recording and transcription of the BDR's own calls. Calven reads transcripts once they are ingested.
- Logging the objection to the CRM.
- Live competitor news. The battlecard and signals hold what the competitive intelligence agent recorded.
